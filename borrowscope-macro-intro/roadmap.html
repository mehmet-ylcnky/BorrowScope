8. Roadmap

Current Status

The macro has matured significantly from its initial prototype. What began as a simple proof-of-concept for tracking variable bindings has evolved into a comprehensive instrumentation system covering the full breadth of Rust's ownership model. The current implementation handles not just the basics—variables, borrows, and moves—but also the advanced patterns that make Rust unique: smart pointers with their reference counting and interior mutability, concurrency primitives with their cross-thread ownership transfers, and async/await with its complex capture semantics. The configuration system has grown to support over 30 options, enabling developers to tune instrumentation precisely for their needs.

  • #[trace_borrow] attribute with 30+ configuration options
  • Variable binding, borrow, and move tracking
  • Smart pointer tracking (Rc, Arc, RefCell, Cell, Weak, Pin, Cow, OnceCell, MaybeUninit)
  • Concurrency tracking (threads, mutexes, channels)
  • Control flow tracking (loops, branches, break/continue/return, ?)
  • Expression tracking (struct, tuple, array, range, cast)
  • Closure capture tracking
  • Async/await tracking
  • ID-based correlation (linking borrows to owners, clones to sources)
  • Variable filtering with glob patterns
  • Sampling support for reduced overhead
  • Conditional compilation modes
  • Diagnostic warnings for ambiguous patterns

Phased Development

Development follows a phased approach, prioritizing practical utility over theoretical completeness. Phase 1 focused on achieving high coverage through enhanced heuristics and syntactic pattern matching—recognizing that perfect accuracy is impossible without type information, but that most real-world code follows patterns the macro can handle correctly. This phase is now complete, with the macro successfully tracking the vast majority of ownership operations in typical Rust programs. Phase 2 will introduce validation tooling—a cargo borrowscope check command that analyzes instrumented code for potential issues before runtime, catching cases where the macro's heuristics may have failed. Phase 3, if demand warrants, would explore deeper compiler integration, potentially through stable hooks that could provide limited type information to procedural macros.

Near-Term Priorities

The immediate roadmap focuses on tooling and usability improvements that will make ownership visualization more accessible and practical. Validation tooling is the top priority—a cargo borrowscope check command that performs static analysis on instrumented code, identifying patterns likely to produce incorrect traces and suggesting fixes or annotations. IDE integration will bring ownership visualization directly into the development workflow, showing borrow relationships and ownership transfers in real-time as developers write code. Performance profiling will identify hot paths in the tracking infrastructure, enabling targeted optimizations for high-throughput scenarios. Finally, expanded documentation with more examples, tutorials, and use-case guides will lower the barrier to adoption and help developers get the most from the tooling.
