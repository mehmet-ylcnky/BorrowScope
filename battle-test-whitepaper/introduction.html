1. Introduction

The companion whitepaper presents a theoretical analysis of why Rust procedural macros cannot access type information during compilation. It explains the fundamental architectural constraint—macros execute before type checking in the compilation pipeline—and explores six potential solutions with their trade-offs. The paper concludes with a prediction: heuristic-based approaches operating purely on syntax can achieve approximately 70% accuracy, with the remaining 30% requiring either user annotations or deeper compiler integration.

Theory, however, requires validation. This paper presents the results of battle testing the #[trace_borrow] macro against real-world Rust codebases. Rather than constructing artificial examples that might favor or disfavor the macro's capabilities, we selected five production-grade projects representing diverse domains, coding styles, and complexity levels. We instrumented every function in each project and measured what compiles successfully versus what fails.

Research Questions

  • RQ1: What is the actual pass rate of syntactic-only instrumentation across production codebases?
  • RQ2: What error patterns emerge, and how do they correlate with the theoretical type information barrier?
  • RQ3: Which errors are most impactful, and what is the optimal fix priority?
  • RQ4: What pass rates can we expect after implementing targeted fixes?

The results both validate and refine the theoretical predictions. We observe an average pass rate of 80.5%—higher than the conservative 70% estimate but well within the predicted range for enhanced heuristics. More importantly, we identify specific error patterns that map directly to the "concrete manifestations" described in Section 5 of the theoretical paper. The mutable borrow conflicts (ERR-003) appearing in all five projects correspond to the paper's discussion of type-dependent method behavior. The trait implementation mismatches (ERR-012) dominating tokio's errors reflect the syntactic indistinguishability of trait declarations from implementations.

Contributions

  • Empirical validation of theoretical predictions across 14,562 functions
  • Error taxonomy classifying six primary failure patterns with root causes
  • Correlation analysis mapping errors to type information barrier manifestations
  • Prioritized roadmap for fixes with projected improvement metrics
  • Reproducible methodology for ongoing regression testing

This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 describes our battle test methodology, including project selection criteria and the instrumentation process. Section 3 presents aggregate results across all projects. Section 4 develops a taxonomy of error patterns with detailed examples. Section 5 analyzes root causes. Section 6 correlates findings with the theoretical type information barrier. Section 7 proposes solutions with expected improvements. Section 8 concludes with implications for BorrowScope's development roadmap.
